## Or: How to Reward Solving Problems Rather Than Solving Puzzles

## EPIGRAPH

"The prestige system rewards breakthroughs in well-bounded problems. But the mortality occurs in unbounded ones."

## SECTION ONE: THE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE

The Nobel Prize and the ERIE Prize measure fundamentally different things. They operate on different timescales, evaluate different evidence, and recognize different forms of capability.

### The Nobel Criterion

**What it measures:**

Problems that are:
- Mathematically bounded (solvable, with clear endpoints)
- Objectively measurable (accuracy can be quantified precisely)
- Recently solved (recognized within researcher's lifetime)
- Independently verifiable (other labs can reproduce)
- Within established scientific frameworks (extending existing paradigms)

**What it recognizes:**

Ability to concentrate resources on a well-defined problem and produce measurable breakthrough achievement within 5-15 year timescales.

**What it ignores:**

- Problems where solution requires 30+ year timescales
- Problems that expand as they're understood
- Problems where success is context-dependent and locally-embedded
- Problems requiring institutional coordination rather than technical insight
- Problems that are "solved" but where the solution has minimal real-world impact

### The ERIE Criterion

**What it measures:**

Problems that are:
- Real-world consequential (actual humans cease suffering or achieve capability)
- Structurally intractable (cannot be reduced to mathematical optimization)
- Empirically dependent (requiring long-horizon observation and local validation)
- Embedded in institutional context (requiring coordination across multiple actors)
- Boundary-crossing (integrating multiple domains that are typically siloed)

**What it recognizes:**

Ability to identify which problems actually matter, restructure how they're framed, build coordination mechanisms that allow solution, and demonstrate real-world impact measured in outcomes that matter to people in specific contexts.

**What it prioritizes:**

Choosing the right problem to solve, not merely solving it brilliantly.

### The Homology

```
NOBEL PRIZE ARCHITECTURE:
├─ Problem is well-defined ──→ Solution is measurable
├─ Solution is measurable ──→ Prestige is assignable
└─ Prestige is assignable ──→ Recognition is unambiguous

ERIE PRIZE ARCHITECTURE:
├─ Problem is real-consequential ──→ Solution is locally embedded
├─ Solution is locally embedded ──→ Impact is context-dependent
├─ Impact is context-dependent ──→ Recognition requires ongoing assessment
└─ Ongoing assessment ──→ Recognition evolves as evidence accumulates
```

The Nobel Prize recognizes fixed achievements. The ERIE Prize recognizes evolving systems.

## SECTION TWO: HISTORICAL ANALYSIS—CASES WHERE NOBEL AND ERIE DIVERGE

### Case 1: AlphaFold vs. Protein Dynamics

**Nobel Prize 2024:** Demis Hassabis, John Jumper, David Baker for "computational protein structure prediction"

**What Nobel recognized:**
- 70-90% accuracy in predicting three-dimensional protein geometry from amino acid sequence
- Closure of 50-year information gap between known sequences and known structures
- Clear benchmark achievement, independently verifiable, measurable

**What ERIE would examine:**

Four questions:

1. **Does this solve actual protein-function prediction problems?**
   
   Answer: Partially. Static structure prediction tells you the most probable shape of a frozen protein. It tells you almost nothing about:
   - How the protein moves at body temperature
   - Which drugs will bind to it in living cells
   - How mutations alter disease risk
   - Why viruses escape immune recognition

2. **What real-world problems does this enable?**
   
   Answer: Some drug discovery acceleration (15-20% speedup in lead compound selection), some structural biology education. Limited impact on disease understanding or treatment development.

3. **What real-world problems does this obscure?**
   
   Answer: The more important unsolved problem—predicting protein dynamics and kinetic behavior—remains invisible. Infrastructure, talent, and funding concentrate around static structure prediction. Dynamic modeling systems remain underfunded.

4. **What would need to be different for this to be real impact?**
   
   Answer: Integration with wet-lab validation showing that predicted structures actually correlate with:
   - Drug binding affinity in living cells
   - Mutation-induced disease pathways
   - Immune escape trajectories
   
   This integration has not occurred. AlphaFold remains a tool in a pipeline, not a solution to a real problem.

**ERIE Assessment:**

Breakthrough in a measurable subdomain that creates the illusion of progress on the larger problem while the larger problem remains unsolved. Potential harm: AlphaFold success absorbs resources that should flow toward actual protein dynamics modeling.

**Prestige lag:** 3-5 years until labor market and research community recognize that static structure prediction was less important than believed.

### Case 2: Bundibugyo Outbreak Response vs. Institutional Credibility Restoration

**Nobel Prize recognition:** Not applicable. Epidemic response does not fit Nobel Prize criteria (ongoing process, context-dependent outcomes, success is multivariate).

**What happened:**

February 2026: Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak begins circulating. Standard diagnostic assays fail. By May, 77-80 days of undetected transmission.

International response mobilized: $200+ million in resources, 500+ international health workers, treatment centers established, vaccination programs launched.

By August 2026: Outbreak had circulated to 4,200+ confirmed cases, likely 6,000-8,000 actual cases. Community distrust at 60-80% saturation. Vaccination uptake below 30%. Treatment center avoidance rate 66-70%.

**What institutional systems measured (Nobel-equivalent thinking):**

- Cases detected: 4,200 (measurable)
- Contacts traced: 40,000 (measurable)
- Healthcare workers deployed: 500+ (measurable)
- Vaccination doses administered: 150,000 (measurable)
- Treatment beds available: 1,200 (measurable)

**What actually determined outcomes (ERIE assessment):**

- Institutional credibility with communities: 20-30% (not measured)
- False-belief saturation in population: 60-80% (not measured)
- Community willingness to present for treatment: 30% (not measured)
- Trust trajectory: declining (not measured)
- Behavioral cascade stability: locked in resistant mode (not measured)

**ERIE diagnosis:**

Institutional response succeeded at measurable metrics while failing at the metrics that actually determine whether outbreak is controlled. By August, all unmeasured metrics showed system failure. Measured metrics showed success.

By September 30, 2026 (estimated): Outbreak would show one of three trajectories:

**Scenario A (25% probability, requires credibility restoration by August 15):**
- 5,000-6,500 cases
- Exponential growth plateau descending
- Trajectory: Containment achieved

**Scenario B (50% probability, baseline prestige-driven response):**
- 8,000-12,000 cases
- Exponential plateau phase continuing
- Trajectory: Crisis plateau, not containment
- Institutional response measured as "successful" (cases detected, resources deployed) while actual outbreak persists

**Scenario C (20% probability, security or funding collapse):**
- 12,000-18,000 cases
- Multi-site escalation (Kinshasa spillover)
- Parallel Marburg outbreak
- Trajectory: Healthcare system functional collapse

**ERIE recognition would accrue to:**

Researchers and institutional designers who:

1. Predicted the false-belief cascade (identified institutional credibility as binding constraint)
2. Designed credibility-restoration interventions (acknowledged past institutional failures, reformed systems)
3. Built community-embedded monitoring systems (measured trust in real-time)
4. Reframed response from "detect and isolate" to "restore and coordinate"
5. Validated interventions through actual health outcomes (community trust recovery, voluntary presentation, successful isolation)

These interventions are unmeasurable by Nobel Prize standards. They are measurable by ERIE standards: Did community trust increase? Did presentation rates increase? Did outbreak trajectory bend downward?

### Case 3: Wobble Position Codon Optimization

**Nobel Prize recognition:** Not applicable (applied biotechnology, lacks prestige cache)

**ERIE case study:**

The wobble position—the third nucleotide of a codon—enables genetic code degeneracy. Multiple codons specify the same amino acid by varying at the wobble position.

**Real-world consequence:**

Viruses that manipulate wobble position codon usage can evade diagnostic assays while maintaining identical protein sequences. During Bundibugyo outbreak, the virus underwent 12+ weeks of natural selection specifically for codon patterns that escape RT-PCR detection. By May 2026, rare-codon variants dominated in undetected transmission chains.

This is not theoretical. This is optimization occurring in real time under selection pressure. Diagnostic assays exerted selection pressure. Viral populations evolved escape variants.

**What this means:**

Understanding codon optimization is not an academic puzzle. It determines:
- Diagnostic escape capacity
- Viral evolutionary trajectories
- Vaccine target stability
- Genome editing success rates (CRISPR efficiency depends on codon usage)
- Biotechnology manufacturing (synthetic biology depends on codon optimization)

**ERIE recognition would accrue to:**

Researchers who:

1. Understood wobble position as forty-four dimensional error-correcting code
2. Predicted diagnostic escape through codon frequency analysis
3. Validated predictions against viral evolution data
4. Designed countermeasures (assays robust to codon variation, vaccines targeting codon-invariant epitopes)
5. Integrated insights into practical diagnostic and therapeutic design

**Nobel Prize recognition would not accrue** because:
- Problem is applied, not fundamental
- Boundaries are unclear (applied to multiple domains)
- Timescales are ongoing (not a solved problem)
- Context-dependence makes generalization difficult

This is exactly the category of work ERIE Prize recognizes and Nobel Prize misses.

## SECTION THREE: THE ERIE PRIZE ARCHITECTURE

### Evaluation Criteria

The ERIE Prize evaluates on seven dimensions:

**1. Problem Selection (25% weight)**

Does the work identify a problem that:
- Causes actual suffering or capability loss in specific populations
- Has been systematically ignored or misframed by existing institutions
- Cannot be solved by conventional application of existing knowledge

Scoring:
- **5 points**: Problem is novel, well-framed, recognized by communities affected but not by institutions
- **3 points**: Problem is recognized but misunderstood by mainstream frameworks
- **1 point**: Problem is recognized and conventionally framed

**2. Structural Understanding (25% weight)**

Does the work demonstrate:
- Understanding of how institutional, behavioral, or technical systems create and maintain the problem
- Identification of leverage points where change is possible
- Integration of multiple domains (psychology, sociology, engineering, biology) that are typically siloed

Scoring:
- **5 points**: Multi-domain integration producing novel framework nobody else recognized
- **3 points**: Solid understanding within existing paradigms
- **1 point**: Incremental advance on conventional understanding

**3. Real-World Implementation (25% weight)**

Has the work been:
- Deployed in actual contexts where it affected outcomes
- Validated through multiple independent implementations
- Shown to produce measurable change in the problem domain

Scoring:
- **5 points**: Multiple implementations, measurable outcomes, replicable across contexts
- **3 points**: Single implementation, validated outcomes, context-specific
- **1 point**: Designed but not yet implemented at scale

**4. Persistence Under Adversity (15% weight)**

Has the solution:
- Remained effective when institutional or behavioral opposition emerged
- Adapted when initial implementation failed
- Demonstrated robustness across varying conditions

Scoring:
- **5 points**: Survived opposition, adapted, replicated, became self-sustaining
- **3 points**: Survived initial challenges, replicated in controlled conditions
- **1 point**: Succeeded in optimal conditions only

**5. Scalability Demonstration (5% weight)**

Can the solution:
- Be applied beyond the initial context
- Be implemented by actors without specialized training
- Maintain effectiveness at 10x scale

Scoring:
- **5 points**: Clear pathway to massive scale with maintained effectiveness
- **3 points**: Scaled to 10x with some efficacy loss
- **1 point**: Requires continued specialist intervention

**6. Irreversibility (5% weight)**

Has the solution:
- Changed institutional structures such that reversion is institutionally difficult
- Altered how communities understand the problem such that regression is unlikely
- Produced durable change even if active maintenance stops

Scoring:
- **5 points**: Became institutionalized; reverting would require deliberate effort
- **3 points**: Maintained by continued effort; would regress if support stopped
- **1 point**: Requires continuous active maintenance

### The Scoring Matrix

```
Criterion              Weight  Max Points  Calculation
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Problem Selection      25%     5 points    × 25% = 1.25 max
Structural Understand  25%     5 points    × 25% = 1.25 max
Real-World Implement   25%     5 points    × 25% = 1.25 max
Persistence           15%     5 points    × 15% = 0.75 max
Scalability            5%     5 points    × 5%  = 0.25 max
Irreversibility        5%     5 points    × 5%  = 0.25 max
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TOTAL                  100%               = 5.0 max score
```

A perfect ERIE Prize entry scores 5.0 out of 5.0.

An entry scoring above 4.0 is recognized. An entry above 3.0 is nominated.

### Recognition Structure

**Tier 1 Recognition (Score 4.5+):**

A single annual award of $10 million. Recognizes work that has measurably changed the trajectory of a major real-world problem across multiple contexts. Annual nomination.

Examples of Tier 1 calibration:

*Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT)* - Created by Norbert Hirschhorn and Robert Crane (1970s), demonstrating that simple salt-glucose solution could prevent cholera death as effectively as IV fluids. Deployed globally, replicated in millions of implementations, became institutionalized in medical practice, saved millions of lives. Irreversible: reverting would require deliberate institutional rejection.

*SCORE: 4.8* (Problem selection: 5; Structural understanding: 5; Real-world implementation: 5; Persistence: 5; Scalability: 4; Irreversibility: 5)

*Conditional Cash Transfer Programs* - Showed that structured cash payments to families meeting health conditions (children vaccinated, mothers receiving prenatal care) produced both immediate poverty reduction and behavioral change toward health-seeking. Deployed in 40+ countries, adapted to local contexts, scaled from pilot to national programs, changed institutional understanding of poverty interventions.

*SCORE: 4.6* (Problem selection: 5; Structural understanding: 4; Real-world implementation: 5; Persistence: 5; Scalability: 5; Irreversibility: 4)

**Tier 2 Recognition (Score 3.5-4.4):**

$2 million annual recognitions. Multiple awards possible. Recognizes work that has demonstrated real-world impact across 2+ contexts, showing clear effectiveness but with some scalability or persistence questions remaining.

**Tier 3 Recognition (Score 3.0-3.4):**

$500,000 annual recognitions. Recognizes work showing promise, with initial implementation successful but scale or persistence not yet demonstrated.

### What Disqualifies

An entry is ineligible for ERIE Prize if:

1. **It is primarily theoretical** without demonstrated real-world implementation
2. **It measures success only by academic metrics** (citations, publications, journal impact)
3. **It creates dependency on external specialists** rather than enabling local actors to solve their own problems
4. **It produces prestige without impact** (famous researcher name without measurable outcomes)
5. **It required perfect conditions** to succeed (cannot function in resource-constrained or adversarial environments)

### The Annual Nomination Process

1. **Open nomination** (any person, organization, or community can nominate)
2. **Community validation** (affected communities must affirm that the work actually addressed their problem)
3. **Multi-disciplinary review** (panels include practitioners, affected community members, researchers, institutional designers—not exclusively academics)
4. **Outcome verification** (actual measurement of real-world change, not projected impact)
5. **Public deliberation** (finalists are public; community feedback shapes final decision)

## SECTION FOUR: WHAT WOULD CHANGE IF ERIE PRIZE BECAME INFLUENTIAL

### Immediate Reallocation (Years 1-2)

If ERIE Prize gained prestige equivalent to Nobel Prize, institutional reallocation would occur:

**Funding flows:**

From:
- Pure mathematics without application
- Theoretical physics without experimental validation
- Structure prediction without integration with dynamics
- Academic publishing without real-world implementation

To:
- Institutional design for complex coordination
- Behavioral change interventions validated at scale
- Integrated systems combining multiple domains
- Implementation science and deployed research

**Talent reallocation:**

Researchers currently building careers around prestige metrics would reorient toward impact metrics. The person spending 8 years optimizing a machine learning benchmark would instead spend 8 years building a system that solves a real problem in a real context.

This would reduce the prestige of certain academic domains and increase the prestige of applied work. The status reversal would occur gradually over 3-5 years as younger researchers orient toward ERIE Prize eligibility.

**Institutional reform:**

Universities and research institutions would restructure incentive systems to align with ERIE Prize criteria. This means:

- Promotion criteria would weight real-world impact alongside publication metrics
- Research centers would require community partnerships and outcome measurement
- Career advancement would require demonstrated implementation, not just theoretical contribution
- Graduate training would emphasize problem-framing and implementation alongside technical skills

### Medium-term Consequences (Years 2-5)

**Convergence on real-world problems:**

Domains that had fragmented between "pure" and "applied" would reconverge. Protein dynamics research would integrate with drug development. Behavioral economics would integrate with institutional design. Network science would integrate with organizational implementation.

This convergence would occur because ERIE Prize recognizes integrated work as more valuable than siloed expertise.

**Reduction in prestige inflation:**

Benchmark competitions (Imagenet, GLUE, SuperGLUE) that measure narrow accuracy metrics would lose prestige. Prizes would shift toward outcomes: "Did this actually help people?" rather than "Did this achieve highest benchmark score?"

This would reduce investment in narrow optimization and redirect resources toward broad problem-solving.

**Emergence of community-embedded research:**

Research institutions would develop permanent presences in communities experiencing real problems. This is expensive and requires long-term commitment, but ERIE Prize recognition would make this investment valuable.

Researchers would spend years understanding local conditions, developing solutions collaboratively, implementing and iterating. This timescale (10-20 years) is currently penalized by academic incentive systems but would be rewarded by ERIE criteria.

**Expansion of implementation science:**

A new discipline would emerge: the science of making things work reliably in real conditions. This is currently underfunded and low-prestige. ERIE recognition would elevate it to equivalent status as basic science.

Researchers would study: What conditions allow interventions to persist? How do solutions adapt to local contexts? How do you scale without losing effectiveness?

### Long-term Structural Change (Years 5-20)

**Prestige bifurcation:**

The prestige system would split into two branches:

**Pure Science Prestige** (Nobel Prize equivalent):
- Recognizes fundamental understanding
- Rewards elegant theory and clean experiments
- Timeframe: 10-20 years to recognition
- Evaluation: Peer review in academic networks

**Applied Impact Prestige** (ERIE Prize equivalent):
- Recognizes real-world problem-solving
- Rewards demonstrated outcomes
- Timeframe: 3-5 years to recognition
- Evaluation: Community and practitioner review

Both paths would be high-status, but they would have different timescales, different evaluation criteria, and different research processes.

This is actually a healthy development: it creates space for researchers who care about foundational understanding (pure science) and researchers who care about real-world implementation (applied impact) to both pursue their values and receive recognition.

**Redefinition of "productive capability":**

The labor market would recalibrate what it pays for. Researcher capability measured as "can this person solve problems people actually face" would command premium over researcher capability measured as "can this person achieve high benchmark scores."

This would manifest as wage divergence: implementation specialists would earn more than pure researchers (currently the reverse).

**Institutional reform in education:**

Universities would restructure graduate training around implementation rather than publication. Instead of "publish 3 papers per year," the requirement would become "deploy 1 solution per 3 years in real context."

This would reduce the prestige of academic publishing as a proxy for capability and increase the prestige of demonstrated impact.

### What Would NOT Change (Structural Constraints)

Some aspects of the research enterprise would remain unchanged because they reflect fundamental tradeoffs:

1. **Prestige lag** would persist but would become shorter. Evidence networks would still update faster than prestige networks, but ERIE Prize's emphasis on real outcomes would reduce the lag from 24-36 months to 12-18 months.

2. **Elite concentration** would persist. Not all researchers have equal access to problems or resources. High-social-capital researchers would still benefit from that capital. But the form of elite advantage would shift: from "can publish in prestigious journals" to "can implement in challenging contexts."

3. **Path dependence** would persist. Researchers who built careers under the old system would not automatically reorient. Career sunk costs are real. But new researchers entering the field would orient toward ERIE Prize eligibility from the start.

## SECTION SIX: THE COMPATIBILITY QUESTION

### Can ERIE Prize and Nobel Prize Coexist?

Yes. They measure different things and operate on different timescales.

**Nobel Prize remains valuable for:**
- Recognizing fundamental breakthroughs in well-bounded domains
- Maintaining incentives for elegant theory and clean experiments
- Preserving space for basic research without immediate application pressure
- Acknowledging scientific achievement when understanding is advanced

**ERIE Prize would be valuable for:**
- Recognizing when understanding is actually deployed to solve problems
- Maintaining incentives for integration across domains
- Preserving space for implementation and scale without academic prestige pressure
- Acknowledging impact when real-world change occurs

The two prizes would operate in complementary domains. A researcher could receive Nobel Prize for fundamental breakthrough and never receive ERIE Prize. A researcher could achieve ERIE Prize recognition without Nobel-level theoretical contribution.

Both would be high-status, high-value recognitions. They would just recognize different forms of excellence.

### The Prestige Architecture Under Coexistence

```
RESEARCH CAREER PATHS UNDER DUAL-PRESTIGE SYSTEM

Pure Science Path:
Year 1-5:  Posoc work → First major paper
Year 5-10: Build research group → Multiple foundational papers
Year 10-20: Lead field → Consistent contribution to understanding
Year 20-30: Recognized leader → Potential Nobel Prize

Applied Impact Path:
Year 1-5:  Identify problem + community partnership → Initial implementation
Year 5-10: Scale implementation → Refine design → Measure outcomes
Year 10-15: Multi-site deployment → Outcome validation → ERIE Prize nomination
Year 15-25: Sustained impact → Institutional integration → ERIE Prize recognition

Dual Path:
Year 1-5:  Theory + implementation partnership
Year 5-10: Theory published + pilot deployment
Year 10-15: Theory mature + mid-scale implementation
Year 15-20: Both validated → Potential for both Nobel + ERIE Prize recognition
```

The dual path is rare but possible. It would represent researchers who both advance fundamental understanding and demonstrate real-world application.

## SECTION NINE: THE DEEPER ARCHITECTURE

### Why ERIE Prize Matters Philosophically

The Nobel Prize was created to recognize genius. It succeeded brilliantly at that goal. But genius at solving well-bounded problems is not the same as wisdom about which problems matter.

An ERIE Prize system would recognize something different: the ability to identify which problems actually matter and actually solve them in real contexts with real people.

This is a different form of capability. It is not lower than the Nobel Prize capability. It is orthogonal to it.

### The Problem Taxonomy

Not all problems are created equal. They differ in fundamental ways:

**Wicked problems** (problems that resist clean solutions):
- Problem definition changes as you understand it
- Solution affects other systems in unexpected ways
- No clear criterion for "solved"
- Example: How to restore institutional credibility in epidemic response?

**Puzzle problems** (problems that have clean solutions):
- Problem is well-defined before you start
- Solution can be verified independently
- Clear criterion for success
- Example: Can we predict protein structure from sequence?

**Wicked** and **Puzzle** problems require different approaches:

| **Wicked Problems**     | **Puzzle Problems**     |
|:---:|:---:|
| Problem-framing critical | Problem already framed |
| Solution iterative | Solution linear |
| Success context-dependent | Success universal |
| Implementation matters as much as theory | Theory sufficient |
| Expertise diffuse | Expertise concentrated |
| Long timescales | Shorter timescales |
| Multiple valid solutions | Single best solution |

The Nobel Prize is optimized for puzzle problems. It excels at recognizing when a puzzle has been solved.

The ERIE Prize would be optimized for wicked problems. It recognizes when actual real-world change has occurred on problems that resist clean solutions.

Most problems that actually determine human welfare are wicked problems. The Nobel Prize system has systematized the recognition of puzzle solutions. We need to systematize the recognition of wicked problem solutions.

### The Recognition Gap

There is currently an asymmetry:

- Puzzle problems get enormous prestige recognition (Nobel Prize, major funding, media attention)
- Wicked problems get minimal prestige recognition (considered "applied," low-prestige)

But wicked problems determine more human welfare than puzzle problems. Institutional design determines more deaths than protein structure prediction. Community credibility determines more outbreak severity than diagnostic technology.

ERIE Prize would correct this asymmetry by elevating wicked problem solutions to equivalent prestige status.

```
  3. Implementation determines outcome quality
  4. Context-dependence is fundamental
  5. Success is durable only if locally embedded
  6. Expertise required is integrative, not specialized

The Prize rewards:

  • Correct problem identification
  • Structural reframing enabling solution
  • Real-world implementation
  • Adaptation to local conditions
  • Durability under adversity
  • Scalability without loss of effectiveness
  • Institutional integration enabling persistence
```

The choice is whether institutional recognition systems will adapt proactively or reactively.

## EPILOGUE: THE MEASUREMENT PROBLEM RESOLVED

The original question: Why doesn't the highest prestige equip someone to solve the highest-stakes problems?

**Answer:** Because the prestige system measures the wrong thing.

It measures achievement at well-bounded problems. But the highest-stakes problems are unbounded. A prestige system that measures bounded problem achievement will systematically undervalue and underfund unbounded problem solutions.

An ERIE Prize system would measure something different: whether a real-world problem that affected real people has actually improved.

Both measurements would be valuable. Both would be high-prestige. They would just recognize different capabilities. The person brilliant at solving puzzles (Nobel Prize) is not the same person as the person brilliant at restructuring wicked problems (ERIE Prize).

We need both. The question is whether our institutions are designed to recognize both.
